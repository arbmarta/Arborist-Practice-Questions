Disorders & Disturbances

Biotic agents are living causes of tree disorders. Understanding the host, causal agent, environment, and pattern of injury helps narrow the diagnosis.

Biotic agents

Biotic agents are living and include pests and infectious pathogens such as fungi, bacteria, viruses, and related organisms. They are often limited to particular hosts and may begin in isolated parts of a plant.

Pests

A pest may compete with desirable plants, threaten plant health or structural integrity, diminish appearance, or reduce utility and enjoyment of the landscape. Pest groups highlighted in the course include borers, leafminers, aphids and scales, mites, nematodes, and other animals.

Borers

Look for evidence such as emergence holes or galleries as well as the tree's response to disrupted tissues.

Sap-feeding pests

Aphids, scales, and mites can produce foliage symptoms, but the organism or other direct evidence is more diagnostic than the symptom alone.

Diseases

The lecture introduces blights, vascular wilts, phytoplasmas, and rusts. Disease diagnosis should combine host identity, signs and symptoms, distribution, site conditions, and—when needed—laboratory analysis.

The plant disease triangle

Disease development is framed through the interaction of the plant, the causal organism, and a conducive environment. A susceptible host and a pathogen do not automatically produce severe disease if environmental conditions do not support disease development.

Alternate hosts and invasive species

Some disease cycles involve alternate hosts. Invasive pests and pathogens can be especially damaging because host trees may have little resistance and introduced pests may lack effective predators. The course notes quarantine zones as a common management response in Canada and the United States.

Check: Why is accurate host identification an early diagnostic priority?

Many biotic problems are host specific. Correct identification can quickly reduce the list of plausible pests and pathogens.
