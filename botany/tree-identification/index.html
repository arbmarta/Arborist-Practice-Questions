Taxonomy as an identification framework

Taxonomy classifies organisms according to natural relationships. The hierarchy becomes increasingly specific from domain through kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, and species. Organisms grouped more closely generally share more morphological features.

Scientific names

A species name combines a genus with a specific epithet, such as Pinus ponderosa. Common names can vary, so scientific names provide a more consistent reference.

Conifer clues

Useful traits include foliage arrangement, attachment, bark, and cones. Fir (Abies) foliage is generally single, flat, and blunt, with upright cones. Spruce (Picea) foliage is generally single, stiff, and sharp, attached on woody pegs, with hanging cones. Pine (Pinus) needles are commonly grouped in fascicles and the cones are woody.

Broadleaf identification

Look at overall form and bark, then examine branch or leaf alignment. Leaves may be simple or compound. Useful leaf characters include shape, margin, tip, base, texture, and smell. Flowers may occur singly or in inflorescences, and fruits can be highly diagnostic.

Identification is strongest when several independent traits point to the same taxon rather than relying on one feature.
