Arboriculture Operations

How tree structure, loads, defects, targets, consequences, mitigation, support systems, and lightning protection fit into tree risk management.

Loads, strength, and response growth

Trees respond to their environment by adding wood where mechanical strain occurs. Response growth includes reaction wood, flexure wood, and woundwood. Loads combine forces; when a load exceeds the strength of a tree part, failure occurs. Gravity is a common static force, while wind produces dynamic loading.

Decay and defects

Decay can reduce wood strength. The material distinguishes white rot, brown rot, and soft rot and emphasizes inspection for structural indicators such as poor taper, root failure, codominant branches, cankers or wounds, cracks, decay indicators, dead branches, human damage, changes in lean, and root or soil movement.

Risk combines likelihood and consequences

Tree risk assessment considers a tree or tree part, a potential target, the likelihood of failure, the likelihood of impact, and the consequences if impact occurs. Occupancy, target zone, time frame, part size, and distance influence the assessment.

The three assessment levels presented are limited visual for broad screening, basic for detailed visual inspection of a tree and site, and advanced for questions requiring specialized equipment, data, analysis, or expertise.

Management and mitigation

All trees pose some risk and also provide benefits. Management involves deciding what level of risk is acceptable and selecting mitigation. Mitigation can be tree-based—such as pruning or health improvement—or target-based. Residual risk remains after treatment.

Support systems

Support options include bracing, static cabling, dynamic cabling, props, and guying. Dynamic systems permit movement and can be non-invasive but require inspection and maintenance. Static systems and bracing can provide durable support but commonly require drilling. Any support system changes how a tree moves and must be installed and inspected according to the system manufacturer's recommendations.

Lightning protection

Lightning can split stems, damage tissues, and side-flash to nearby structures. Protection systems are intended to provide a preferred conductive path toward ground and require specialized design, installation, and inspection.
