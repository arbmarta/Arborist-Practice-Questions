Arboriculture Operations

Selecting the right tree for the site, evaluating nursery stock, planting correctly, and supporting establishment after transplanting.

Right plant, right place

Selection begins by matching site conditions with plant traits. Consider climate, moisture, exposure, soil pH, texture, volume, salinity, compaction and organic matter, along with utilities, pavement, structures, crown-clearance needs, future development, and maintenance access.

Plant traits include hardiness, heat tolerance, preferred soil, mature height and spread, root system, pest and disease vulnerability, maintenance needs, and nuisance or ornamental attributes.

Planting stock

Common stock types include bare-root, balled-and-burlapped, containerized, container-grown, and in-ground fabric bag stock. Each has tradeoffs in cost, handling, planting window, root condition, and size. Inspect the root system and locate the trunk flare, the transition from trunk to structural roots. Encircling or girdling roots can impair vascular function and future growth.

Planting

Moisture availability is a central timing concern. The planting hole should be shallow and wide, with the trunk flare at ground level. The source material recommends retaining on-site backfill soil and a hole approximately twice the root-ball diameter. Avoid routine amendments and fertilization without a soil-based reason.

Staking and mulch

Most trees do not require staking, but it may be useful on windy sites, for small root systems, poor trunk taper, or large evergreen crowns. Supports should allow some movement and must be removed before they girdle the tree. Mulch is presented at about 2–3 inches deep, extending outward where practical and kept away from the trunk.

Establishment

Irrigation is the most important establishment treatment. Monitor and adjust for species, exposure, soil type, and drainage. Early pruning should focus on dead, diseased, and broken branches; avoid heavy live-crown removal while the tree is recovering from transplanting.
