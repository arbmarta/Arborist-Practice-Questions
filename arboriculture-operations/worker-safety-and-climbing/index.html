Arboriculture Operations

Core jobsite safety concepts, communication, work zones, electrical awareness, climbing terminology, rope systems, and emergency response.

Personal protective equipment

Core PPE includes head, eye, and hearing protection, with task-specific additions such as cut-resistant leg protection for chainsaw work and manufacturer- or regulation-directed protection for pesticide work. Clothing should permit movement without being loose enough to snag.

Job briefings and work zones

A job briefing coordinates who will do what, identifies hazards and controls, identifies required PPE, and establishes the communication system. A work plan should include emergency contacts, the nearest medical facility, and emergency transportation information.

The work zone is the overall area affected by the job. A drop or landing zone is where cut material is expected to fall or be lowered and must be clear before cutting.

Electrical, fuel, and equipment hazards

Treat electrical conductors as potentially carrying fatal voltage. Workers must be trained before using equipment or performing assigned tasks. Fuel spills should be cleaned before engines are started, smoking is prohibited around flammable liquids, and fuel must be stored in approved containers away from ropes and equipment.

Climbing terminology

Tensile strength is the breaking strength of new rope or hardware. Working-load limit is the highest intended load. A design factor relates breaking strength to intended working load. A knot is tied in one rope, a hitch secures a rope to an object or rope, and a bend joins rope ends.

In a moving rope system (MRS), the climbing line moves through the tie-in point as the climber moves. In a stationary rope system (SRS), the climber moves along a stationary line.

Emergency response

Assess site hazards and injuries, summon emergency medical services for emergencies, use first-aid PPE, and manage contaminated materials appropriately. Severe bleeding requires rapid control; the source material distinguishes direct pressure from tourniquet use depending on severity.

Safety note: This study page summarizes course concepts. Actual tree work requires current training, applicable regulations, manufacturer instructions, and job-specific procedures.
